How search works

The local all-MiniLM-L6-v2 sentence encoder turns catalogue text and a query into 384-number vectors. FAISS compares normalized vectors, then lexical title, alias, author, and tag matches influence the result order. Exact tag filters narrow the result set. This encoder is not a text-generating LLM.

How tiers adapt

Each reader's ratings and chosen or confirmed tiers form a weighted baseline. Matching tags and reading state adjust a saved title's tentative tier. When you rate or move a book, NoveList recalculates only your still-tentative placements. Your direct choices stay yours.

What the system does not claim

It does not train a new neural model for each reader or recommend unseen books. There is no judged search evaluation yet, so match scores and tier suggestions are aids to exploration, not measured predictions of what you will enjoy.

Questions

How NoveList works

How are tentative tiers chosen?

Direct placements and ratings establish your baseline. Shared tags and the title’s reading state adjust that estimate. Dropped titles start conservatively.

What teaches the ranking model?

Rating, dragging, assigning a tier, or confirming a suggestion records feedback. Later predictions use those choices, with more weight on completed and dropped titles.

Why might a cover be missing?

A source may not publish one, may block hotlinking, or may have moved the image. NoveList tries its local cache, a safe proxy, and the source URL before showing a placeholder.

Can readers add novels?

Readers can request a title. Administrators can run exact-title discovery or broader collection jobs so the shared catalogue stays controlled and deduplicated.
